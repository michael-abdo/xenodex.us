THE ATTEMPTS

  • 1. The Elimination Standard
  • Rigorous personality-genetics research must systematically rule out:

Why The Elimination Standard Matters

The Problem: Personality genetics research has a massive replication crisis. Studies find correlations, make headlines, then fall apart when other researchers try to repeat them.

Why This Keeps Happening: Researchers find real statistical correlations between physical traits and personality, but they're measuring the wrong thing. They think they've found genetic programming when they've actually found:

  • Cultural stereotypes that became self-fulfilling prophecies
  • Environmental factors that affected both body and mind
  • Population differences that mix genetics with culture
  • Indirect genetic effects rather than direct programming
  • Biased measurement methods

The Stakes: If we can't distinguish real genetic patterns from these confounds, we're just creating sophisticated astrology. Worse - we might make important decisions (education, medicine, policy) based on correlations that aren't actually genetic.

What Rigorous Science Requires: Before claiming any personality-genetics connection, you must systematically rule out these alternative explanations:

1. Cultural/Social Conditioning - "People treat redheads like they're fiery, so redheads learn to act fiery"

  • Society has expectations based on how you look
  • You unconsciously adapt to meet those expectations
  • What looks like genetic personality might just be learned behavior

2. Developmental Environment - "Poor nutrition affects both your height AND your brain development"

  • Things like diet, toxins, stress during childhood affect your whole development
  • Your personality and physical traits might both come from the same environmental cause
  • Not genetic - just shared environmental damage/benefits

3. Population Stratification - "Italians are expressive because of Italian culture, not Italian genes"

  • Different ethnic groups have different cultures AND different genetics
  • Hard to tell if personality differences come from genes or culture
  • Maybe all the correlations are just cultural, not genetic

4. Gene-Environment Interactions - "Your genes make you seek out certain experiences, which then shape your personality"

  • Genes might not directly control personality
  • Instead, they influence what environments you choose or how you react to them
  • The environment then shapes your actual behavior

5. Methodological Artifacts - "You only studied college students, who aren't representative of everyone"

  • Problems with who gets included in studies
  • Problems with how traits are measured
  • Researcher bias in interpreting results

The Standard: A method that can't eliminate these alternatives isn't doing genetics research - it's just finding patterns that could have multiple causes.

  • Multiple independent studies
  • Publishable in respected journals

  • 1 hr long stream of consciousness over 5 week periods

1. Standardized Protocols

  • Exact script for session setup/prompts
  • Consistent duration (60 minutes, standardized breaks)
  • Controlled environment (quiet room, no distractions)
  • Training manual for administrators across sites

2. Blind Analysis

  • Behavioral coders never see participants' photos
  • Physical trait measurements done by separate team
  • Data merged only after both analyses complete
  • Double-blind where possible

3. Inter-Rater Reliability

  • Multiple coders analyze same sessions independently
  • Statistical agreement requirements (>0.8 correlation)
  • Clear coding rubrics for each behavioral pattern
  • Regular calibration sessions between coders

4. Cross-Cultural Replication

  • Minimum 3 distinct cultural contexts
  • Same protocols adapted for language/culture
  • Local research teams to avoid experimenter bias
  • Pre-registered hypotheses for each cultural context

5. Statistical Controls

  • Age, education, socioeconomic status as covariates
  • Multiple testing corrections for pattern analysis
  • Effect size reporting (not just p-values)
  • Confidence intervals for all correlations

6. Falsification Criteria

  • Pre-registered predictions about which traits should correlate
  • Clear thresholds for what constitutes replication failure
  • Null results publication commitment

7. Open Science Practices

  • Data sharing (with privacy protections)
  • Code transparency for all analyses
  • Replication materials publicly available

This creates a methodology so rigorous that objecting requires either finding specific technical flaws or revealing non-scientific motivations.
