Methods & Reproducibility

Pre-registration, frozen estimators, and open data & code

Pre-Registration

The directional prediction was committed in writing before the held-out re-run, in two public Open Science Framework pre-registrations:

  • OSF 10.17605/OSF.IO/SMRXT — held-out cohort, n = 83.
  • OSF 10.17605/OSF.IO/KJ2RW — powered replication, n = 549.

A single frozen estimator — a whitened linear discriminant on the full 40-dimensional Basel Face Model shape coefficient vector, with no per-component selection — was fingerprinted with SHA-256 and timestamped on OSF, then run unchanged on cohorts the discovery analysis never touched. Cross-validation is identity-disjoint (one row per person, leakage-free folds by construction), and the shape vector is sex-residualized — biological sex is read from the annotation record, never inferred from the image — so the apparatus cannot simply re-read demographic sex from the face.

Pre-registered directional prediction · single frozen estimator (whitened-LDA on the 40-dim BFM shape vector) · SHA-256-fingerprinted & OSF-timestamped · identity-disjoint cross-validation · sex-residualized · tested on n = 83, then replicated on n = 549.

Freezing the estimator and the cohort removes the analytic flexibility — post-hoc estimator re-selection, cohort swapping, substitution of discovery numbers — that makes face-to-trait results so frequently irreproducible. The held-out outcome was committed to be reported verbatim, whichever way it fell.

Label-Leakage Hygiene

A second, standalone methods paper addresses the shared-provenance failure mode. When a text channel — an automatic-speech-recognition transcript — and its label both descend from one recording, naive text-to-label prediction scores well for reasons unrelated to the construct: the model re-reads the cues the annotator read, or reads the construct's own jargon spoken aloud. The number is tautological, not evidential.

We quantified this on a real corpus of 87,943 transcript clips across 370 human-labeled subjects, decomposing the contamination into separable leakage and quality channels. The dominant contaminant is attribution: 78.3% of clips are not the subject's speech but two narrator/host voices talking about them, dropped wholesale by narrator person_id. Within the 13,060 remaining real-subject clips, four further channels apply (the flags overlap, so they are reported separately rather than as a disjoint partition):

ChannelEffect on the real-subject clips
Narrator / host speech (Filter 0)78.3% of the full corpus — two host voices talking about the subject; dropped wholesale by person_id
Typology-jargon leakage10.47% leak construct jargon — the transcript literally contains the label
Host third-person commentary2.72% are narration about the subject mis-attributed to them by the diarizer
Within-person near-duplicates18.39% are re-cuts or re-uploads that inflate the sample and let one phrasing win a vote
Too-short backchannel14.89% are sub-sentence fragments (≤ 50 chars) carrying no behavioral signal
Survive all filters (CLEAN)61.78% of the real-subject clips pass every filter

Requiring at least 20 clean clips per subject yields a leakage-screened allowlist of 189 subjects / 5,916 clip IDs, down from a raw ceiling of 243. Thirty-seven subjects carried at least 20% jargon and would have silently poisoned a raw benchmark.

87,943 clips · 370 subjects audited → 189 subjects / 5,916 clean clip IDs. 37 subjects (≥ 20% jargon) excluded before they could poison a benchmark.

This is framed as a gate, not a result. It removes the crudest input-layer leakage but cannot remove shared-provenance circularity itself: the label was still assigned by a human watching the same recording. A strong text-to-label score therefore stays ambiguous until it is paired with a cross-modal or independent-text guard — predicting from one channel while labeling from another, so the signal can no longer be a single-channel tautology.

Data & Code Availability

The frozen artifacts and both pre-registrations are public. The analyses are reproducible from the OSF-timestamped, SHA-256-fingerprinted estimator and the pre-registration records (DOIs 10.17605/OSF.IO/SMRXT and 10.17605/OSF.IO/KJ2RW above). Released material comprises the confirmatory analysis code, the per-coin result tables, the bootstrap and false-discovery-rate outputs, and the leakage-audit allowlists — not the underlying raw media.

The reproducibility ethos: pre-commit the test, freeze the estimator, report the result whichever way it falls.
