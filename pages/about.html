THE RESEARCHER

Xenodex is the work of Michael Abdo, an independent researcher. The program asks a narrow empirical question: whether facial morphology carries a developmental trace—a correlate of the prenatal hormonal milieu that also shapes the brain.

The hypothesis concerns shared developmental origin, not revelation of character. A face does not disclose who someone is, what they value, or what they will do.

The claim is about common cause in development, not about reading character from a face.

THE STANCE

Four commitments define the work. First, a common-cause hypothesis—not physiognomy: any face–trait correlation, if it exists, reflects a shared developmental origin rather than the face causing the trait.

Second, a probabilistic prior, never a verdict: an estimate carries a confidence and an error rate; it does not classify a person.

Third, demographic confounds—sex above all—get controlled before any signal counts as real.

Fourth, and central: report your own nulls.

The program's flagship prediction was pre-registered, then refuted on held-out data. Publishing that null is the point—a result that survives scrutiny matters more than one that flatters the hypothesis.
