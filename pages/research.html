A multi-axis common-cause model — and its pre-registered falsification

The Non-Shared Developmental Residual

Where in the variance budget a developmental cause must live

The largest twin meta-analysis to date (Polderman et al., 2015) aggregated 17,804 traits across 2,748 publications and 14.5 million partly dependent twin pairs — effectively the entire published twin literature on complex traits. It reports a mean heritability of approximately 49%, and for roughly 69% of traits the observed twin correlations fit a parsimonious additive-genetic model, with little contribution from shared family environment.

The implication is structural rather than incidental. For most traits, the variance that is not additive-genetic is also not shared-environmental. What remains is a large non-shared residual: individual-specific variance unexplained by either the genome or the household. Personality heritabilities for the Big Five cluster in a ~41–61% band, with shared environment near zero and the non-shared component absorbing the large majority of environmental variance. Facial morphology shows the same architecture at higher heritability — identical genomes do not produce identical faces.

Distinct from "missing heritability"

This residual must not be confused with the missing-heritability gap. Missing heritability is the difference between twin-estimated and SNP-captured heritability — it is still genetic, living in rare variants, structural variation, or many small additive effects. The non-shared residual sits on the opposite side of the genes / not-genes partition. A directly acting developmental cause can target only one of them; conflating the two would misdirect the search.

This non-shared residual is a plausible — not proven — natural home for a prenatal developmental contribution. Trait-relevant individual differences are observable in infancy, before extended socialization could account for them, and even monozygotic co-twins differ measurably in face shape. The synthesis establishes consistency, not confirmation: it narrows the search space rather than closing it.

≈ 49% mean heritability · ~69% of traits fit an additive model · the remainder is largely non-shared, individual-specific variance — the candidate home for a prenatal developmental contribution.

A Multi-Axis Common-Cause Model

Form as a correlated trace, not a cause

One axis of this account is already accepted science. Prenatal androgen exposure acts as a common cause of co-occurring physical and behavioral traits: it jointly influences digit ratio, spatial cognition, and the anatomical-behavioral profile of congenital adrenal hyperplasia. A hormone measured in umbilical-cord blood at birth predicts the sexual dimorphism of the adult three-dimensional face two decades later (Whitehouse et al., 2015). One gradient, two correlated outputs — a body and a brain, built together.

The model generalizes this single-axis precedent. Across critical fetal windows, the intrauterine hormonal milieu — testosterone, cortisol, thyroid, estradiol and others, each varying in dose and timing — simultaneously shapes facial structure and neural architecture. Writing the developmental input as a vector h of hormone exposures, the model posits two coupled maps from the same argument: a morphological map M(h) to adult form, and a neural map N(h) to dispositional architecture. Because both read the same h, their outputs correlate with no causal arrow between body and mind. Observable form is a correlated trace of the developmental event that shaped disposition — not a cause of it.

The falsifiable prediction

A model earns serious attention only if it forbids something. Define, for each trait-axis a, its hormonal organization O(a) — how strongly that axis is specified by h. The model predicts that the readability of an axis from facial morphology increases monotonically with O(a). This is a hierarchy prediction, not a point prediction, and it is falsifiable three ways: a flat ordering implies no common-cause coupling; an inverted ordering implies the model is wrong; and a weakly organized axis that reads strongly implicates an alternative cause such as a demographic artifact. No prior physiognomic claim made an ordering prediction; they claimed uniform legibility — precisely the form that confounds counterfeit.

Predicted readability ordering, frozen before the test: function ≥ sex-modality > letters. The distinctive bet: the function axis carries the largest readout after biological sex is residualized out.

The Pre-Registered Test — and Its Refutation

A claim made precise enough to be killed by its own data

An exploratory discovery cohort of 707 identities appeared to support the prediction: a function-axis effect of Cohen's d ≈ −0.47 (sex-residualized ≈ −0.45). But that analysis selected, per axis, the maximum-effect principal component — exactly the inflation that pushes small effects toward whatever hierarchy is hoped for, in the same |d| ≈ 0.4–0.5 range where individual facial-width-to-height-ratio lab studies over-read before the field's pooled effect fell to d ≈ 0.22. The discovery result was therefore declined as confirmation.

An adversarial confirmatory design

Instead, an adversarial test was pre-registered: a single frozen whitened-LDA estimator consuming the full 40-dimensional Basel Face Model shape vector — no per-component selection — with identity-disjoint cross-validation and sex-residualization (biological sex read from a database record, never inferred from the image). The estimator, the held-out cohorts, and the blind feature arrays were timestamped on the Open Science Framework with SHA-256 fingerprints, foreclosing post-hoc estimator re-selection, cohort swapping, and substitution of the discovery numbers. The directional decision rule named, in advance, what would count as refutation.

The hypothesis was refuted on 83 held-out identities, and refuted again on a powered replication of the byte-identical estimator across 549 identities. The observed sex-residualized ordering inverted the prediction:

Observed: sex-modality |d| = 0.39 > a letter axis (S_N) |d| = 0.27 > function |d| = 0.21 > the other letter axis (F_T) |d| = 0.12. The flagship function axis ranked below a letter axis and failed FDR correction (p = 0.17); a letter axis predicted to lie at the null instead survived.

The one robust survivor — the sex-modality axis (d ≈ 0.39, p = 1.1 × 10⁻⁵, the only effect whose bootstrap interval excluded zero) — is the axis most tightly tied to biological sex. Because the adjustment projects out only the linear sex direction, a residual that survives there is most parsimoniously read as a sex-correlated demographic shortcut: the classic physiognomy failure mode in which a face→trait signal reduces to a demographic proxy. The discovery's magnitude is largely excluded, while a genuinely small function effect (d ≈ 0.20) remains undetermined — and, given exhaustion of the rare lead-class in the typed population, unanswerable here without a major new typing-and-photography effort.

Reading the Receipt, Not the Person

The bright line between common-cause inference and physiognomy

The result earns a sharp distinction. Physiognomy asserts body→mind causation and uniform legibility of character — a verdict recoverable from any face. The common-cause claim asserts a shared upstream cause, predicts a graded ordering of readability that tracks an independently known measure of hormonal organization, and yields only a probabilistic prior to be updated — never a verdict about an individual. The face is the receipt of a transaction you cannot observe directly, not the transaction itself.

The discipline that separates the two practices reduces to three commitments, each disarming one of physiognomy's failure modes:

Three commitments

(1) Pre-register the directional prediction — commit the hypothesis, estimator, covariates, and decision rule to a timestamped record before touching held-out data, foreclosing the silent switch from the component that failed to the one that worked. (2) Control for demographic confounds, sex above all — read sensitive demographics from outside the image, residualize, and replicate within each stratum; an effect that vanishes under sex adjustment was never a trait read but sex, read. (3) Honestly report your own nulls — specify in advance what failure looks like, and when it happens, report the refutation rather than reaching for the slice that rescues the headline.

This program ran the second practice on its own most-wanted result and lost. Reporting that null under pre-registration — rather than burying it — is itself the evidence that the line between the two claims is real.
